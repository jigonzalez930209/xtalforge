# Powder indexing

## Peak positions to reciprocal space

Every observed line becomes `Q = 1/d^2 = (2 sin(theta) / lambda)^2`. A metric
family predicts `Q = h^2 A + k^2 B + l^2 C + h l D` with one coefficient per
free parameter:

| Family | Parameters | Form |
|---|---|---|
| cubic | 1 | `Q = (h^2+k^2+l^2) / a^2` |
| tetragonal | 2 | `(h^2+k^2)/a^2 + l^2/c^2` |
| hexagonal | 2 | `4/3 (h^2+hk+k^2)/a^2 + l^2/c^2` |
| orthorhombic | 3 | `h^2/a^2 + k^2/b^2 + l^2/c^2` |
| monoclinic (unique b) | 4 | `h^2/(a^2 sin^2 b) + k^2/b^2 + l^2/(c^2 sin^2 b) - 2 hl cos b/(a c sin^2 b)` |

## Solving a family

A family with `k` parameters is solved from `k` observed lines whose `hkl` are
enumerated up to `max_index`; the resulting linear system is solved by Gaussian
elimination. The first lines of a family are often degenerate for this solve
(a tetragonal pattern starts with `h00` and `hk0`, both with `l = 0`; the first
four monoclinic lines do not reach the cross term), so `k+2` lines are offered
and the singular subsets are skipped. Monoclinic tries four of the first six.

A candidate cell is kept when:

1. the metric parameters are positive and the cell is inside the length,
   angle and volume bounds;
2. it predicts the next unused observed line within the tolerance;
3. it indexes at least `min_completeness` of the pattern.

The metric is then refined by linear least squares on the matched reflections,
in windows that grow from the unambiguous low-angle lines to the whole
pattern, with re-matching between passes. The window matters for large cells:
one wrong high-angle assignment would otherwise pull the cell far enough to
lose the rest of the pattern.

## Figures of merit

`M20` (de Wolff) and `F_N` (Smith and Snyder) are computed from the same
prediction:

- `Q20` is the reciprocal squared of the twentieth observed line (or the last
  one when fewer), `N20` the number of calculated reflections in one hemisphere
  with `Q <= Q20`, and `<|dQ|>` the mean absolute difference over the matched
  lines among those twenty;
- `M20 = Q20 / (2 <|dQ|> N20)`;
- `<|d2theta|>` is the mean two-theta difference over the same lines and
  `N_possible` the number of calculated reflections up to the last observed
  line; `F_N = N_observed / (<|d2theta|> N_possible)`.

Both count reflections in one hemisphere (the factor two fills the sphere) and
both grow with the number of predicted lines, so a cell that predicts far more
lines than the pattern shows is not rewarded. `coverage` is the fraction of
those predicted lines below `Q20` that the observed peaks explain, which is
what separates a real cell from a large overfit cell.

## Ranking

The candidates are ordered by:

1. coverage class: at least 95% of the observed lines indexed counts as full
   coverage (the rest is an impurity tolerance);
2. the metric family, highest symmetry first;
3. `M20`, then the smaller cell volume, then `F_N`.

A supercell indexes the same lines with a worse figure of merit; it is removed
when its `M20` is below one tenth of the best cell in its coverage class. The
symmetry preference is what makes a cubic pattern come back cubic instead of
one of the many monoclinic descriptions of the same lattice.

## Validation

`crates/xtalforge-analysis/tests/indexing_golden.rs` reads
`tests/golden/phase-7/indexing/cells.json`, generated by
`scripts/golden/indexing.py` from five known cells (cubic Si, tetragonal TiO2,
hexagonal ZnO, orthorhombic MFI and a synthetic monoclinic cell). The peaks
are the Bragg positions up to 100 degrees with a deterministic 0.006 degree
jitter, and the golden file stores M20/F_N computed by an independent Python
implementation of the definitions above. The Rust test checks that:

- `figures_of_merit` on the true cell reproduces the Python M20, `F_N`, `N20`
  and `N_possible` to `1e-4` relative;
- `index_pattern` finds a candidate with the same lattice (Q spectra agree to
  `5e-4` relative) in the expected family, with at least 95% coverage.

The unit tests add the analytic dispersion of each family and the option and
input validation.

## Limits

- The solving lines must have indices within `max_index` (default 3); a cell
  whose first observed line has a larger index needs a larger budget.
- The per-family work is capped by `max_evaluations` (default 20,000 solved
  assignments), so the search is deterministic and bounded on a dense pattern.
- A cell and a supercell of the same family with systematic absences can both
  index a short peak list; the ranking prefers the smaller cell, but a pattern
  without the distinguishing reflections cannot separate them.
- The M20/F_N definitions follow the conventions stated above; other programs
  may count `N20` slightly differently, which changes the absolute value but
  not the ranking.
