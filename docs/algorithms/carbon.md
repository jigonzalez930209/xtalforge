# Disordered carbons

## Convolution surface

A net (`dia` or `pcu`) is repeated over the recipe's supercell, its vertices
and edge midpoints are distorted by a seeded draw, and every edge is sampled
with beads 0.3 Angstrom apart. The field is the sum of Gaussians of width
`0.5 R` over those beads, not a distance to the nearest edge: four struts
add smoothly at a node, while a distance field creases there. The marched
level is the field a distance `R` from an endless row of beads has, so along
an edge the sheet is a tube of radius `R = radius_ratio * edge_length`.

The periodic grid spans one cell including both faces, and the marching
tetrahedra output is welded by fractional position. The sheet is closed and
its Euler characteristic is `2(V - E)` of the net quotient.

## Remesh

The fine sheet is remeshed towards an even triangulation:

1. split every edge longer than the upper threshold,
2. collapse every edge shorter than the lower threshold, refusing a collapse
   that would pinch the sheet or turn a triangle over,
3. flip towards valence six,
4. move each vertex tangentially to its neighbours and project it back onto
   the level set with a capped Newton step.

The target is 3.55 Angstrom, which settles the dual's bonds near 1.50
Angstrom. The reference aims at 2.46 Angstrom and reaches 1.42 Angstrom
bonds; the difference is documented below.

## Ribbons

The recipe keeps a fraction of the sheet, not the whole tube network. Each
net edge gets a shortest path over the triangle graph from the anchor of one
net vertex to the anchor of the other; the anchors are shared, so the paths
form the net's own connected network. The kept carbon is the set of graph
nodes nearest that network, grown until the largest piece holds the carbon
count the density asks for and percolates in all three directions.

## Dual and termination

One carbon sits at every kept triangle centre, bonded across every shared
edge; the graph is three-coordinate by construction. Edge carbons (fewer than
three neighbours) receive the recipe's hydroxyl, carbonyl, fluorine and
hydrogen counts, with the ethers taking a carbon's place. Edge carbons beyond
the requested ratios are left bare and said in the notes.

The structure states its bonds explicitly. The ring census reads those stored
bonds (`ring_counts_explicit`), so a close contact across a pore is never
perceived as a bond.

## Recipe and report

`CarbonRecipe` holds the net, repeat, density, radius ratio, coverage, layer
count, distortions, Stone-Wales rate, termination ratios and seed. `build_carbon`
returns `CarbonBuild` with the structure, ring census, carbon density, H/C,
F/C and O/C, edge fraction, mean C-C bond, pieces, periodicity, closed-sheet
carbon count, bare edges and notes.

## Validation

`tests/carbon_golden.rs` reads `tests/golden/phase-6/carbon/dia.json`,
generated by `scripts/golden/carbon.py` from Crystal-Builder's recipe builder
(diamond 1x1x1, 0.42 g/cm3, 38 % coverage, seed 0, relaxation off). The RNG
and the remesh differ, so the test is statistical:

- atom count within 10 % (reference 420),
- carbon density within 0.03 g/cm3,
- H/C, F/C and O/C within 0.03,
- edge fraction within 0.08 (reference 0.43),
- one piece percolating in three directions,
- mean C-C between 1.25 and 1.56 Angstrom,
- closed-sheet carbon count within 15 %,
- six-membered rings are the mode, and the total ring count is at least half
  the reference.

## Documented deviations

- The seeded RNG is not numpy's, so the atom list is not identical.
- The remesh target is chosen for 1.50 Angstrom dual bonds; the reference's
  2.46 Angstrom target gives 1.42 Angstrom. The density and the counts still
  agree within the tolerances above.
- Stone-Wales pairs are accepted by `CarbonRecipe` but not placed.
- Only one layer is supported; a multi-layer recipe is refused.
- The ribbon path is a shortest path over the triangle graph, not the
  reference's noise-weighted spine.
